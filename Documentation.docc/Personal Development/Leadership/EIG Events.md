# EIG Events and Technical Subcommittees

Running events focused on technical STEM concepts for NP students

## Overview

The Engineering Interest Group (EIG) is an events-based co-curriculuar activity that runs engineering-related 
workshops and competitions.

As a member of the Events subcommittee, I was responsible for:
- Writing event proposals
- Preparing procurement forms for event logistics
- Creating event materials, such as slides and scripts
- Running dry-runs
- Executing the event itself
- (For competitions) managing the judging and prize process

I later transferred to the Technical subcommittee, which was similar to the Events subcommittee but focused
more on the technical content taught during workshops.

## Egg Drop Competition

My first event was an Egg Drop competition, held after EIG's annual general meeting. It involved a currency system,
where teams could use points (including bonus points from the AGM) to purchase materials to protect their egg from
a drop at height. One twist we added was a "shark tank" mechanic, where teams could come up to the judges and pitch
their ideas - teams would be given extra points for creative and well-presented pitches.

My team originally consisted of myself and two others, however one left midway through. One major achievement of this
event was that it ended on time, despite starting 10 minutes late - this speaks to our ability to plan for disruptions 
ahead of time.

One obstacle we had to overcome was choice of venue. Our original plan was to drop the eggs from outside the lecture 
theatre, however the area underneath was already booked for a separate event. We ended up using another building slightly 
further away, and had to rearrange some plans to account for the increased travel time to and from the venue.

## Figma Workshop

Figma is a software primarily used for digital design. Figma was supposed to be my first workshop, however it was 
delayed from early 2026 to mid 2026. My team consisted of three others, all from different courses.

Figma was difficult to teach because the user interface has a lot of options. Teaching it fully in-slides was not
feasible, because we needed to show interactions between the cursor and the design elements. Teaching it fully in-Figma 
was not desirable either, because certain parts of the interface had dozens of options that would need explanation.

We settled on a hybrid method, where parts of the UI would be explained in slides first, then demonstrated using an app. 
This approach was reused for the Figma workshops in IdeateComm, though run by other people.

## Arduino Workshop

Arduino is a cheap microcontroller that is common in hobby electronics. The workshop focused on using the Arduino to
create a programmable car, capable of moving forward, backward, and braking autonomously. I was a helper in this event
rather than a planner, meaning that my involvement was limited to just the dry runs and running the workshop itself.

One challenge we encountered was that the components used on the day itself were different from the components used for 
testing - specifically, the exact model of Arduino was different. Unknown to us, the model used for the Arduino had lower
power limits than the one we had tested on. This meant that the Arduino was not capable of powering the motor unless 
the motor was already moving, as it takes up to 4x the power to start a motor than to keep it going.

We solved this issue by instructing participants to give the cars a small push to overcome the starting power requirements.
However, the issue still delayed the event by 30 minutes as we debugged the problem.

## Excel at Excel Workshop

Excel is the spreadsheet application created by Microsoft, and is common in Engineering contexts as it makes it easy to
calculate and manage many values at once. I was a helper in this event rather than a planner, meaning that my involvement 
was limited to just the dry runs and running the workshop itself.

Since this was purely a software workshop, and Ngee Ann uses Microsoft for its office suite, the workshop ran very smoothly.
This Excel workshop significantly scaled back the amount of content taught due to feedback from prior years, however some 
participants this year felt that it had now become too simple. Dialing in the difficulty was a major challenge.

## IdeateComm Competition

IdeateComm is an annual community-focused competition run by EIG. The first day of the competition is the opening and 
workshops, and the subsequent 3 days are the actual hackathon days. I was responsible for leading the 3D Modelling Workshop
on day 1 and helping to run the hackathon on days 2-4. I led part of the dry runs for Day 1 due to my experience with 
<doc:NP-Ambassadors>, focusing on making travel and transitions between different segments as smooth as possible.
