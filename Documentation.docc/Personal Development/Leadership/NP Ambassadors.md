# NP Ambassadors

Being the public face of Ngee Ann Polytechnic

## Overview

Ngee Ann Polytechnic (NP) Ambassadors are a student extension of the Corporate Communications Office, and 
are responsible for running large public-facing events at the Polytechnic level, such as Open House, RED 
Camp, and Youth Leaders Camp.

I was an NP Ambassador from 2025 to 2026.

## RED Camp

Roadmap to Explore and Discover (RED) Camp is NP's flagship event for graduating Secondary 4, Secondary 5, 
and ITE students. Students get to explore different academic schools, explore the campus and hear from current 
NP students about student life.

Running RED Camp involved coordinating with student leaders from the different academic schools, as the 
programmes for each school was run by their own student leaders and not Ambassadors.

One surprising thing about preparing for RED Camp was the amount of time spent practicing moving between venues, 
which is usually a part of the event hand-waved away. This is because Ngee Ann is a large and hilly campus, and
large groups of students can get lost, break away from the group, or slow down at tight corners, causing event delays.
This taught me about paying attention to small details in an event.

## Red Camp Boot Camp

Red Camp Boot Camp (RCBC) is a preparation camp for the recruited Student Leaders prior to RED Camp. I was
a member of the Product Knowledge (PK) subcommittee, in charge of content for the School of Film and Media Studies.
This meant I needed to know the content of that school incredibly well, such as each course's target audience, 
curriculum, specialisations, industry connections, and career prospects.

The PK Subcommittee was also brought in as extra manpower for the RCBC Games. I learned to collaborate with people 
from all eight academic schools, manage tight deadlines, and run games at the scale of hundreds of participants.

## Open House

My role in Open House was a secondary school tour guide. I would lead groups of secondary school students to 
a lecture theatre for a presentation, then to one academic school.

When I was not leading secondary school students, I was a member of the Welcome Team, greeting people entering the 
campus. I am not an extrovert, so this level of public engagement forced me to be more confident with interacting 
with strangers.
