My leadership experience, what I've learned, and how I've grown

## Overview

I am currently the inaugural Team Manager of Ngee Ann Polytechnic's Drone Club, leading Drone Club in building,
testing, and crashing drones for the Singapore Amazing Flying Machine Competition. I am also in the <doc:EIG-Events>
subcommittee, running STEM-based workshops and events for polytechnic students, and the <doc:TCP-Events> subcommittee,
helping NP students reach their fullest potential.

Previously, I've led teams in various capacities throughout the <doc:Apps> and <doc:Firmware-Projects> I've worked on. I
was in <NP-Ambassadors>, running large public-facing events at the Polytechnic level. In secondary school, I led my 
school's App Development Talent Development Programme, <doc:SST-Inc>, for two years, as <doc:SST-Inc-Chief-Technical-Officer-iOS>
and later <doc:SST-Inc-Vice-President>. I had to manage an executive committee of 12 students, and the Programme, with 
over 100 students. To effectively lead in all of my positions, I had to develop my communication, organization, and 
problem-solving skills.

## Running Events

A lot of my leadership positions included running events. I have run events of different scales, from dozens to thousands
of participants; at different levels, from helper to Overall In Charge; and topic, from STEM-heavy workshops to the generalist
Open House.

I especially like running workshop/sharing and competition-style events. I believe that I am in the position I am in
because of the skills I have learned, and that I should share the same with others.

## My People Philosophy

When I'm working with other people, I like to identify what people are best at. For example, if somebody is good
at talking to people - why waste their talent by shoehorning them into an administrative role? In general, unless
the person wants to take on a different role, I believe people work best when allowed to work to their specific strengths.

With respect to doing work, I believe that work is only valuable insofar as it works towards your own passions. In
polytechnic, plenty of people sign up for work because it is related to what they are interested in. Their work _is_
the passion, and I make sure to be wary that nobody is ever pushed to the point where their workload just becomes
work. Importantly, I ensure full-day sessions do not start before 10 and end after 4, and that everyone is free to
voice out when they feel stressed.
